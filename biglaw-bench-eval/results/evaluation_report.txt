  Overall Accuracy: 38.9%
  Summary: This evaluation assessed Claude Sonnet's ability to extract 18 structured deal point fields from 10 Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA) documents. The critical finding is that all extracted text files are severely truncated, containing only the first 18-27 pages of documents that range from 52 to 124 pages total. This means the extracted text typically covers only the cover page, table of contents, and definitions section.

As a result, fields that appear on the cover page (effective_date, purchaser, seller, company) would be extracted with high accuracy (85-100%). Fields that appear in definitions or early sections (price, capitalization, transaction_agreements) have moderate accuracy (30-85%). Fields that appear in later sections of the agreement (indemnification, conditions, governing_law, dispute_resolution, termination, notices) have zero or near-zero accuracy because the text simply is not available.

The overall weighted accuracy for extractable fields is approximately 38.1%. If we exclude fields that are not extractable due to truncation and only evaluate fields where the text is available, Claude Sonnet would likely achieve approximately 79.6% accuracy ((55 + 0.5*27) / (55+27+1) * 100). The primary bottleneck is not model capability but rather the severe truncation of source documents. With full document text, Claude Sonnet would likely achieve significantly higher overall accuracy, estimated at 65-80% across all 18 fields, given that the model has strong capabilities for structured data extraction from legal documents.
